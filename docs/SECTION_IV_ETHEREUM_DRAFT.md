# IV. ETHEREUM-BASED IMPLEMENTATION — Working Manuscript Draft

> Editorial state: source-grounded comprehensive draft, not yet condensed to the six-page article. Baseline contract functions were inspected directly. The Phase 4 rewards/agreement functions are a new uncompiled research extension and must remain labeled as such until Foundry tests pass.

## A. Ethereum Environment, Identity, and Registration

SSE separates blockchain consensus from contractual service monitoring. Its deployment architecture targets permissionless Ethereum, where an execution client validates EVM state transitions, a consensus client tracks the proof-of-stake chain, and an optional validator client participates in block proposal and attestation. Application participants need not act as validators: consumers, providers, monitors, and settlement executors transact through an Ethereum RPC endpoint using locally controlled accounts. The inspected prototype uses Anvil with Ethereum-compatible execution and local block production; it does not demonstrate independent Ethereum proof-of-stake consensus or a publicly accessible, permissionless validator network.

Participants are identified through Ethereum addresses rather than a centralized provider registry. A consumer can simultaneously provide resources in another lease, whereas the same address cannot represent both counterparty roles within a single lease. Authentication of the relevant off-chain instructions uses EIP-712 typed signatures with a contract- and chain-bound domain. The settlement contract recovers the signer of provider acknowledgments, provider response commitments, and monitor votes. These signatures establish attribution and message integrity; they do not establish physical receipt, service correctness, or delivery time. A traditional X.509 certificate authority is not required for Ethereum account registration, although TLS authentication may separately protect provider-service endpoints.

Monitoring candidates register by depositing a minimum bond in `OpenMonitorCommittee`. Each funded lease requests an eligibility snapshot excluding the consumer and provider. The prototype samples four addresses using a seed supplied by a configured randomness authority; all four selected accounts must confirm before the lease reaches the `Ready` state. Three matching authorized reports suffice for a monitoring classification. This vote threshold is an application policy and not Ethereum consensus. Because the registry imposes a 32-identity lifetime capacity and the randomness seed is not verified on-chain, the current implementation is a bounded monitoring adapter rather than the approved scalable, verifiably selected monitoring system. Bond-backed open enrollment does not, by itself, prove organizational independence or prevent Sybil control.

## B. Smart-Contract Architecture and Financial State

The implemented protocol is organized around three cooperating contracts. `OpenResourceBoard` registers resource requests and provider bids and permits the consumer to select a bid and atomically fund a settlement lease. `OpenMonitorCommittee` maintains bonded monitoring candidates, assignment snapshots, selected addresses, and bond locks. `SSESettlement` manages escrow-backed leases, epoch admission, typed-message authentication, evidence acceptance, settlement bounds, and withdrawable credits. The independent `SSEBoundsPure` library expresses the restricted N/F/S calculation for verification; the legacy `OpenMonitorBondPool` is not part of the active lease-to-settlement path.

During setup, the committee pool and resource board are bound to their settlement contract through one-time deployment functions. A marketplace-funded lease commits the consumer and provider identities, resource-profile and SLA-policy hashes, endpoint commitment, epoch fee, reservation entitlement, evidence deadlines, exposure budget, and maximum number of epochs. Its initial deposit equals the maximum service fee liability for the configured epochs. The direct-lease entry point remains available for isolated experiments but does not include the marketplace policy hash. After the provider accepts and monitoring assignments are confirmed, the consumer may admit a service epoch if uncommitted escrow is sufficient and the full additional epoch fee fits within the remaining exposure budget.

Service funds are accounted for in contract storage; beneficiaries receive withdrawable credits rather than direct transfers during settlement. Network gas charges are recorded by Ethereum itself and must not be confused with contractual monitoring compensation. The Phase 4 source extension introduces an optional, separately funded incentive budget for one fixed reward per eligible monitor report and one-time bounties for successful sealing, settlement, evidence closure, and committee release. Reward rates and maximum liabilities are funded before provider acceptance, and unused reserved compensation can be returned once ordinary monitoring obligations end. These additions have been written as source code, but their execution remains unverified until the new Solidity tests pass.

## C. Evidence Processing and Epoch State Transitions

Each admitted epoch records the consumer's unique request commitment and a deterministically derived expected-response commitment. The contract defines three successive checkpoints: the response deadline, monitoring close, and evidence close. The provider may submit an EIP-712 acknowledgment before evidence closure and an authenticated response commitment before monitoring close. Selected monitors perform service observations off-chain, retain full measurement records externally, and submit authenticated verdicts accompanied by evidence hashes. A signed verdict identifies the reporting account and binds a record commitment, but the external observation remains subject to the monitoring soundness assumptions.

The monitoring contract recognizes `Success` when at least three selected monitors submit matching success reports, `Failure` when at least three submit matching failure reports, and `None` otherwise. Under the fixed model, settlement begins with the admissible histories N (unverified receipt), F (received but failed), and S (fulfilled). A verified provider acknowledgment excludes N; an accepted success classification retains S; and an accepted failure classification excludes S. An empty history set causes a revert rather than an arbitrary payout. For every nonempty admissible set, `settleEpoch` calculates the minimum and maximum contractual provider payments, credits only newly guaranteed provider and consumer amounts, and retains the difference in escrow. Because prior payments are not reversed, the algorithm rejects an evidence update that would invalidate already credited entitlements. The same transition decreases the lease's outstanding exposure as the admissible payment interval narrows.

Anyone may call the deterministic settlement functions when their preconditions hold. `sealMonitoring` is eligible after monitoring closes, and `closeEpochEvidence` seals and settles an epoch if necessary after the evidence window expires. The consumer may close the lease to further admissions and reclaim unused service escrow; selected monitoring bonds are released after all admitted evidence windows close. Unresolved portions remain separate from guaranteed credits and do not become payable merely because a timeout has elapsed. The additional `resolveResidualByAgreement` implementation, once validated, will permit exact allocation of a closed epoch's residual only when both consumer and provider sign the same EIP-712 agreement. Such funds are transferred by bilateral consent, not by evidence proving a unique outcome.

## D. Prototype Scope and Implementation Constraints

The prototype demonstrates the enforcement-side SSE mechanism over a bounded three-history SLA with EIP-712 identities, escrow conservation, and conservative admission. Recorded local experiments used Anvil, four selected monitoring addresses, a three-vote classification rule, HTTP challenge observations, and a Docker compute workload. The local controller coordinates transactions using development accounts, so these runs are not evidence of independently administered monitors or a deployment across an open public blockchain. Furthermore, provider response signatures authenticate expected response commitments but do not prove that an HTTP response was delivered before the on-chain response deadline.

The broader architecture specifies open-ended monitoring registration, verifiable lease-specific selection, fixed funded rewards, mutual residual allocation, modular service agents, and automatic Ubuntu VM enrollment into a deployed Ethereum application network. Only the funding and residual-allocation components have been added as new, currently unverified Solidity source in this implementation increment; verifiable randomness, an unbounded monitoring registry, independent distributed agents, and automated VM onboarding remain outside the demonstrated baseline. These boundaries are essential when interpreting the financial guarantees: SSE can settle conservatively relative to the accepted admissible histories, but does not remove the need to justify the evidence rules that produced those histories.
